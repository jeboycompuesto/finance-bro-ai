# Financebro demo video script

**Target runtime: 2:50.** This assumes a three-minute maximum and leaves ten seconds of buffer. The opening is 45 seconds, the screen-recorded walkthrough is 115 seconds, and the closing takes ten seconds. Timestamps are edit targets, not a measured read-through.

Inspired by the pacing of the [InHand reference script](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1vxWY2WuJZLzdeq-9FQTQSpuv9Ad0CSrzhvU5jH53rwE/edit?usp=sharing): hook → relatable problem → reveal → demonstration → payoff.

**Draft basis:** Financebro product brief and existing expansion/runway mockup context. The running application has not been verified. Use the expansion flow below if it is ready; match spoken claims to what the recording actually shows. Figures, if used, are illustrative demo data. The InHand Google Doc was read; the linked X video could not be loaded.

## Visual direction

Use the founder-at-a-laptop presentation from the [Cofounder reference](https://x.com/titouangillet_/status/2095521587785081193?s=20) with the original narration and timing below. One founder, one laptop, one quiet location with indirect daylight. The founder addresses the camera during the opening and closing; narration continues over the product footage during the walkthrough.

Establish the founder and laptop together, move over the shoulder as they interact, then cut into a clean screen recording when the audience needs to read. Match the laptop and screen-recording UI states across the transition. Show the actual input before its output, with brief views of the founder watching the work finish. Use readable screen captures for the key results.

## 0:00–0:08 | Hook

**Visual:** Start on a medium three-quarter shot with both founder and open laptop in frame. An editorial message overlay reads “Ready to launch in New York?” The founder glances at the screen, then looks toward camera for “can we actually afford it?” Hold that look briefly. Keep the overlay visually separate from the app UI.

**Founder:**
“We’re ready to expand into New York. One question: can we actually afford it?”

**On screen:** Can we afford our next move?

## 0:08–0:28 | Founder POV

**Visual:** Stay at the same desk. Cut to an over-the-shoulder view of a demo bank balance on “bank balance,” a hiring/launch note on “hire two people,” and a spreadsheet on “checking formulas.” Brief close-up of the founder scanning the sheet on “wondering what I forgot.” Let the narration connect the shots; use fictional data throughout.

**Founder:**
“I can check our bank balance. But that doesn’t tell me what happens after we hire two people, pay the launch costs, and wait for customers to pay us. So now I’m building a spreadsheet, checking formulas, and wondering what I forgot.”

**On screen:** Cash. Costs. Assumptions. One big decision.

## 0:28–0:45 | Product reveal

**Visual:** Return to the founder for “So we built Financebro.” On the product name, cut to a close-up of the laptop displaying the Financebro home screen and wordmark. Show a hand on the trackpad as the narration describes the workflow. On “Let's try it,” move closer to the screen, then cut to a clean recording of that same home screen. This transition lands at 0:45.

**Voiceover:**
“So we built Financebro, an AI financial modeling copilot for founders. Start with a business question. Bring in your financial data, add your plans, and see how the decision could affect your cash. Let’s try it.”

**On screen:** Your next move, modeled.

## 0:45–1:05 | Walkthrough: the starting point

**Visual / action:** Continue in the clean screen recording on the populated dashboard. Establish the whole screen, then gently crop closer to cash, runway, and spending as each is mentioned. Use deliberate cursor movements and preserve the labels. Keep the demo-data label visible when applicable.

**Presenter:**
“We’re using a demo company. Here’s our cash, our estimated runway, and where we’re spending each month. This is our starting point before adding the expansion.”

**Recording cue:** Begin with data already loaded; avoid spending the walkthrough on account setup.

## 1:05–1:25 | Walkthrough: ask the business question

**Visual / action:** Briefly cut over the founder's shoulder as they begin typing. Return to the clean capture for the complete prompt: “Can we expand into New York next quarter?” Show the submit action, then the response. If there is processing time, a short shot of the founder watching the laptop can bridge it before returning to the actual follow-up questions.

**Presenter:**
“I’ll ask: ‘Can we expand into New York next quarter?’ Financebro asks for the details our historical data can’t tell us: what we’re planning to spend, and when we expect the new revenue.”

**Recording cue:** Pause briefly so viewers can read the question and the response.

## 1:25–1:50 | Walkthrough: make the plan explicit

**Visual / action:** Fill in the assumptions requested by the actual build. Prioritize launch cost, additional monthly costs, revenue timing, and minimum cash reserve if those fields exist. Use one brief hands-on-keyboard insert; keep the meaningful edits in readable screen capture. Hold on the completed assumptions before submitting so viewers can connect the plan to the forecast.

**Presenter:**
“Let’s add the launch cost, two new hires, and when customers will start paying. We’ll also set the cash reserve we want to keep. These are our assumptions, so we can change them as the plan develops.”

**Recording cue:** Prepare the inputs before recording. Show one or two meaningful edits; cut repetitive typing.

## 1:50–2:20 | Walkthrough: pressure-test the decision

**Visual / action:** Show the resulting forecast in full-screen capture and compare it with the baseline. On “what if,” briefly cut to the founder looking at the laptop, then return to the input. Change revenue timing to two months later and show the updated forecast, if supported. Otherwise use a working downside scenario. Keep the chart at the same crop and scale across the comparison when possible. Point to the cash low point or runway change and hold the result for at least three seconds. Keep axes and labels visible.

**Presenter:**
“Here’s what the expansion does to our projected cash. Now let’s test one assumption: what if customers pay two months later than we expected? We can see how that changes the forecast, and whether we still have the cash reserve we wanted to protect.”

**Recording cue:** This is the main demo moment. Hold on the before-and-after result. Add one concrete sentence using the actual result: “That moves our lowest cash balance from [shown amount] to [shown amount].” If the UI shows runway instead, use those values. Replace bracketed text before recording; do not invent a result. If using a scenario tab instead of editing timing, replace the question with: “Now let’s look at the downside case.”

## 2:20–2:40 | Walkthrough: understand the answer

**Visual / action:** Open the recommendation and its explanation in the screen recording. Highlight the main condition or risk in the result with a restrained editorial outline. Hold long enough to read it, then return to the founder and laptop together for “what needs to go right.” Their expression should suggest understanding; match it to the actual result.

**Presenter:**
“Financebro explains what’s driving the result and what we should watch before committing. Now we can see the cost of the plan, where it gets tight, and what needs to go right.”

**Recording cue:** Read the recommendation on screen during rehearsal. Replace the general narration with its specific takeaway if it fits this time slot.

## 2:40–2:50 | Closing

**Visual:** Use the original medium shot for “We're building Financebro to help founders understand the financial impact of their next big decision,” with the finished model on the laptop. Cut to the logo and “Your next move, modeled” for the final phrase. Hold the end card for the last two seconds, within the ten-second closing slot.

**Voiceover:**
“We’re building Financebro to help founders understand the financial impact of their next big decision. Financebro. Your next move, modeled.”

## Recording notes

- Capture four reusable camera views: founder with laptop, over the shoulder, hands on keyboard/trackpad, and a straight-on laptop close-up. Keep desk props and laptop position consistent.
- Record the screen separately at high resolution. Match the UI state when cutting between physical laptop footage and the screen recording. Use captured product footage if replacing a filmed screen in editing.
- Use indirect window light or full shade, position the laptop to reduce reflections, and lock camera exposure/focus. Record clean narration and quiet room tone; keep music below speech.
- Place captions clear of inputs, chart labels, and recommendations. Keep transitions simple and let the forecast change be the main visual event.
- Keep one company and one decision throughout the video.
- Record the walkthrough first, then adjust the narration to the exact UI and results.
- If editing assumptions is unavailable, show an available downside scenario. If neither is implemented, omit that interaction and give more time to the working forecast and explanation. Match the narrated inputs to the fields actually available.
- Mention Rho, Plaid, or another connection only if the recording demonstrates it. If showing a connection prototype, identify it as a prototype. The default script uses labeled demo data.
- Cut loading time, add captions, and check the exported video's duration. The timestamps are an editing plan, not a measured read-through.
